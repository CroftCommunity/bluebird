About Bluebird

A bluebird day is ski slang for a clear blue sky over fresh snow: the perfect day to be out on the mountain.

Trail ratings are trust ratings, not content ratings: green is the garden your Patrol tends, blue is one hop beyond it, black is the open mountain.

New here as an explorer? The How it works page is the calm tour.

How setup works

You set up here, on your own device or computer. You do not need the explorer's device in front of you.

  1. Build the explorer's garden on this page — pick the accounts and channels they'll see.
  2. Copy that explorer's link (each explorer card has one).
  3. Send the link to the explorer. When they open it on their device, that device becomes their garden.

The link is safe to send. It carries only public information — where your records live and which record to load. It never contains a password or any secret.

Signing in (optional)

Signing in with Bluesky lets you publish an explorer's settings straight to your account with one tap, so the garden updates without copying anything by hand. It opens Bluesky in the same window, then brings you back here.

You can also skip signing in: set your account (your DID) by hand and save each explorer's record to your account yourself. Either way works.

First, secure your account

A garden is only as safe as the account that tends it. Do these once, before you invite an explorer:

  • Turn on email 2FA for your Bluesky account (Settings, on bsky.social).
  • Harden the email inbox behind it — a strong, unique password and its own 2FA.
  • Know where to revoke device sessions — your account's OAuth session page — so you can cut off a lost device.

What's public and what's private

An explorer's garden settings are stored as public records on the network, like everything on Bluesky. Use a nickname for the explorer — never a real or legal name, school, or age. The explorer's Saves and notes stay private, only on their device.
